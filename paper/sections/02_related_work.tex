\section{Related Work and Working Hypothesis}
\subsection{Task-program development}
SS-Replan~\citep{garrett2020replan} alternates deterministic belief-space
planning, action execution and observation updates, initially reusing the
remaining discrete plan structure. We propose this execution pattern for
stocking, starting with privileged state and bounded order search; our current
prototype does not implement its belief filtering or probabilistic costs.

\citet{merler2026coding} develop reusable programs through simulator interaction
and evaluate frozen policies without test-time LLM calls. Their main setting
withholds environment source; their Shelf case includes kinematic calibration.
The repository distinguishes Dynamic3D Shelf from a preliminary kinematic Shelf
environment~\citep{robocode2026}.

Our hypothesis is that development-time experimentation can help discover
placement orders, approach choices, and recovery rules for MABEL. We will first
use source-visible model-based skills, including IK and gravity compensation.
That access setting differs from the paper's main setting. Supplying engineered
skills also changes what the coding agent must discover; experiments must expose
this choice rather than attribute all improvements to program synthesis.

The main novelty question remains open: does this combination improve complete
shelf completion under tight geometry and physical failures, beyond a competent
hand-written policy with the same controller? The proposed experiments address
that question before any claim about generality or reduced engineering effort.

\subsection{Contact interfaces and matched collection}
\citet{burgess2025get} introduce GET: three tapered fingers in a two-against-one
arrangement driven by one parallel-jaw actuator, with rigid nails and optional
optical tactile sensing. Their teleoperated object/task comparisons motivate
testing contact geometry independently of action dimensionality; they do not
demonstrate autonomous crowded-shelf execution. The associated release includes
UMI-compatible CAD. Three contact fingers must not be confused with three
independently commanded fingers.

Dream Gripper is a separate hardware reference using a printed rigid backbone,
compliant TPU insert and grip tape~\citep{dreamgripper2026}. Its released CAD and
STL files provide a fabrication starting point, not a controlled shelf-stocking
benchmark or a calibrated force sensor. Neither reference establishes the benefit
of the independently actuated hand proposed below; reuse terms and robot mounting
also require verification.

UMI couples handheld collection with a deployable policy interface, including
latency matching and relative trajectories~\citep{chi2024umi}. This motivates
matching the robot and collection tool's contact geometry and timing. It does
not establish transfer for our proposed fingers, sensing or recovery behaviors.
Our working hypothesis is that co-designing contact mechanics, observations,
reactive execution and the collection interface improves complete interactions
at useful task pace. This remains an experimental hypothesis, not a novelty claim.
